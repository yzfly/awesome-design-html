Design System Reference

The tile is the brand.

Apple's web identity is not a color scheme — it is an information architecture. Edge-to-edge product tiles, a centered headline stack, one pair of blue links, and silence everywhere else. The structure does the talking; the accent color only ever says "act here."

View source ↗

Color

Three surfaces, one accent. White and light gray alternate to give the tile matrix its rhythm; pure black appears at most twice for contrast, never a middle gray. Action Blue is the only chromatic voice — on dark tiles it brightens to stay legible, but it never gains a sibling.

Typography

Inter stands in for SF Pro: weight 600 maximum, negative tracking that tightens as size grows. Headlines are large but never bold-shouty — hierarchy comes from size and whitespace, not weight. Everything in a tile is centered; everything in reference sections is left-aligned.

Voice & Tone

Apple copy is a headline discipline: fragments over sentences, product names as subjects, wordplay earned by restraint everywhere else.

Fragments
"All out Pro." "Thinstinct."
Two or three words that sound inevitable. Verbs optional, periods mandatory.
Product as Hero
"iPhone 17 Pro" — never "our new phone."
The product name leads every tile. The company stays out of the sentence.
Numbers, Quietly
"From $999" in fine print, never in the headline.
Specs whisper. Feelings shout. Price is a footnote to desire.
