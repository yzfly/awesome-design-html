Design System Reference

Numbers set like couture

The structural idea of this study: Ferrari's identity does not live in photographs — it lives in figures. So the page treats performance data as the hero asset, set in Didone serifs at display sizes on a near-black stage, with Rosso Corsa rationed like a scarce material and one drop of Giallo Modena.

Color

Typography

Bodoni Moda carries everything that matters: model names, performance figures, italic Italian asides. Inter does the quiet work — labels are small, uppercase and widely tracked, so the serif numerals read even larger by contrast. The figure is the headline; the label is the footnote.

Radius

Edges are machined, not rounded. Surfaces and figures sit at 0; interactive elements get 2–4px so they don't look like print errors; the pill exists only for small tags. Anything softer would betray the carbon.

Voice & Tone

Ferrari copy speaks in declarations, not features. Italian comes first when the emotion matters; English follows like a translation in a museum. Numbers are stated bare — never "up to", never "approximately".

Declarative
"La Ferrari più potente di sempre."
Not "one of the most powerful". The superlative or silence — hedging is for other brands.
Italian First
"Noi siamo la Scuderia."
Italian is the voice; English is the subtitle. Cavalli, not horsepower. Rosso, not red.
Numbers as Poetry
1000 · 2.5 · V12
A figure set large enough needs no adjective. The spec sheet is the love letter.
