iOS Surface Anatomy

Three surfaces. One black pill.

Every Uber screen earns its position with the same grammar — an edge-to-edge map, a sheet that lifts above it, and a single black pill that says "go." Nothing decorative survives.

Where to?

The most-tapped surface in the app. Pill search bar at the top, a 2×2 of shortcut tiles (Home, Work, Saved, Send package), then a horizontal carousel of suggestions over a faded map preview.

Ride Selection

Map fills the top half, a sheet lifts from the bottom with options listed vertically — UberX, XL, Black, Pool. Each row is icon + arrival + ETA + right-aligned price. The full-width pill CTA confirms.

Trip Tracking

Full-screen map, route polyline, driver car icon, pickup and dropoff pins. Bottom card shows driver photo, rating, plate, model, and three action pills: Message (primary), Call, Share Trip.

Dynamic Island · Live Activity

Your ride lives in the notch.

From the moment you confirm a ride to the moment you arrive, Uber occupies the Dynamic Island with a compact car icon, the ETA, and the plate number. Long-press expands to driver photo, "Call/Message" buttons, and a live mini-map.

Lock screen mirrors the same payload through ActivityKit; the car icon stays on the wallpaper.

Pin Selection

Drag the map. The pin stays still.

Uber's pickup-precision moment is one of iOS's most physical UX gestures. The pin is anchored to the screen center; the map slides under it. A black callout above the pin shows the address and updates as the map settles for ~200ms after the last drag.

Each meaningful position change emits a tiny selection haptic — the same one iOS uses on its time-picker wheels.

settle: 200ms haptic: selection pin: 32×42pt
Tip & Rate

Five black stars. Four pill options.

Post-trip is the only place Uber dedicates a full-screen modal. Big bold stars at the top — black, never gold — then a 4-up tip selector ($1 / $2 / $5 / Other) at full-pill radius. The Done button at the bottom is the same pill.

Rating below 5 stars triggers a follow-up sheet asking what went wrong; categories appear as additional pills.

Design System Reference

Black, and the map.

Every Uber token starts at #000. White is the second color. The teal accent shows up so rarely you forget it exists. Maps carry the entire visual hierarchy of the surface.
